About

I go where the data is hard to get, and bring back something people can use.

I'm a Kenyan geomatics engineer based in Johannesburg, finishing an MScEng in Geomatics at the University of Cape Town.

I have more than five years of academic, internship and project experience, and my work runs from field capture to the final deliverable. I've flown drone-mounted ground-penetrating radar over the Fimbul Ice Shelf in Antarctica, scanned campus gates, high-rises and homes with terrestrial and SLAM laser scanners, mapped Robben Island at 1:10 000, and analysed change at a mine site in Google Earth Engine.

I studied Geomatics with Computer Science, so I write the code between the sensor and the map: Python pipelines for radar traces and survey data, LISP routines for BIM workflows, and web viewers that carry an analysis to the people reading it. I've logged 224 days of Work-Integrated Learning toward registration as a Professional GISc Practitioner, and I've studied and worked in Botswana and South Africa.

  • Field to deliverable

    I capture data and process it myself, from raw GPR, LiDAR and photogrammetric data through to survey deliverables.

  • Works without supervision

    I planned and ran my own survey campaign during a three-month season in Antarctica, with very limited outside support.

  • Automate the repeat work

    I extended 5D Geo's TerraScan classification macros and automate repeat CAD and GIS tasks with LISP and Python.
